# Cant style — how a term is coined

This is the **anchor** that keeps every day's term sounding like it belongs to the same city
and the same underworld. Read it every day before coining. (It is the cant's equivalent of a
conlang's phonology: the fixed rule set that makes accreted words feel related.)

## What the cant is
A **cryptolect**: ordinary Sietran words — about water, salt, fish, masks, ivy, crows, glass,
bells — reassigned to secret underworld meanings. To the Watch it sounds like dock gossip; to
those who hold the key, it's business.

## Rules for a good term
1. **A real thing first.** The term must be something Sietrans already say with a plain,
   boring meaning: a bird, a tool, a knot, a fish, a plant, a job, a kind of weather, an hour
   of the day. "A rook" is a bird. "A gaff" is a fishing tool. "The pruner" is a gardener.
   Write that plain meaning down; it goes in the entry as *literally:*. If you cannot state a
   plain meaning a dockhand would recognize, it is not a term yet.
2. **Description is not disguise.** The words must not describe the hidden meaning. "A quiet
   hand" for a card cheat and "blind captain" for a bribed official are descriptions — any
   stranger decodes them on the spot. A real cant term has a missing step: you need the key to
   get from the plain thing to the meaning. If the words alone tell a stranger who or what it
   is, throw it out and pick a different thing.
3. **Never name the crime.** The term must NOT contain, translate, or lightly paraphrase the
   thing it means. If the hidden meaning is "a burglary," the term cannot lean on *break-in*,
   *job*, *take*, or *night* — those already point at theft. Trade words for trade things are
   the same trap: *cargo* for contraband and *a haul* for a score are shop talk, not cant.
4. **Extend one family, inside its domain.** Every term descends from exactly one of the seven
   families in CORE_IMAGERY.md, and its hidden meaning must fall inside that family's stated
   domain. Sharing a word with a family is not enough: noble patronage is ivy, but a crime
   family's patronage is not, because a crime family is not nobility. The `extends:` field is
   the family's exact name.
5. **Match the kind of word to the concept.** A person-concept yields a noun for a person. An
   act yields a verb phrase. An object yields a noun for an object. "A pickpocket" must not
   come out as the theft, and "a rival" must not come out as a rival's operation.
6. **Worn words are banned.** These have been used so often they no longer hide anything.
   Do not use them as any part of a new term: **hand, catch, haul, cargo, cast, hold, mooring,
   line.** Also avoid echoing any noun from the "Recent terms" list in STATE.md — that list
   is what to steer away from, not a style to continue.
7. **Mundane surface.** The term is an innocent noun or short verb phrase an actual dockhand,
   fence, or salt-farmer might say. One word, or a 2–3 word phrase ("open the sluices,"
   "walk the wood," "a sprig for him"). Avoid hyphenated compounds; they read as invented.
8. **Deniable.** A full sentence using the term should still read as harmless small talk.
   Before accepting a term, read its context line aloud and ask: *would a Watch officer at the
   next table hear anything but dock or weather gossip?* If the crime shows through, recoin it.
   The context line must also obey every convention in CONVENTIONS.md: it never names the
   Collective, never names the Watch, and never contains a person's name.
9. **Dry and gallows-humored.** Understated, a little grim, never melodramatic. Sietrans joke
   at the foot of the scaffold.
10. **Grounded.** Prefer Sietro specifics (the Flats, the sluices, the Fratelli piazzas, the
    north wind, the Withered Bloom) over generic thief-slang ("hot piece," "the job," "the take").
11. **English first, Italian as seasoning.** The players speak English, so terms are mostly
    plain English. Reach for an Italian word only when it genuinely fits or gives a coined term
    some flavor (*acqua alta* for the rising Watch, *la bocca* for a denunciation drop, *corvo*
    for a crow/informant). Keep any Italian easy for an English speaker to read and say — a light
    accent, never a whole second language. Most days will have no Italian at all, and that's fine.

## What a lexicon entry looks like
Each day's entry records the **term**, its **hidden meaning**, the **plain meaning** it hides
behind, the **family it extends** (exact name), and one line of it **used in context**. Examples
of the style (do NOT copy these — coin new ones):

- **high water** — the City Watch is out in force — *literally: a spring tide* — *extends: Water & the law* — "Keep the cart home, it's high water tonight."
- **salted** — paid off, bribed into silence — *literally: cured for keeping* — *extends: Salt & labor* — "The gate captain? Salted since spring."
- **a rook** — a lookout — *literally: a roof bird* — *extends: Crows & gallows* — "Put a rook on the north roof."
- **a quiet face** — a forged identity that won't draw questions — *literally: a plain Carnival mask* — *extends: Masks & Carnival* — "She landed in the Ivy on a quiet face."
- **calling the crows** — arranging for a body to disappear — *literally: scattering bread for birds* — *extends: Crows & gallows* — "That debt got settled by calling the crows."
- **the north wind's buying** — the Collective is quietly taking over an operation — *literally: a cold front coming in* — *extends: The north wind* — "Renoldi's old fence? The north wind's buying."

a district/faction variation — that fits the cant so far.
